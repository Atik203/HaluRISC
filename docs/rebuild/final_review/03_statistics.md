# Statistics and Calibration Review

**Manuscript:** `Journal_Paper/halurisc.tex`  
**Build reviewed:** `Journal_Paper/out/halurisc.pdf` (25 pages; Tables 8, 9, 12 and 13 checked against the PDF)  
**Scope:** metric definitions, calibration, uncertainty, paired tests, conformal prediction, numerical consistency  
**Out of scope:** prose style, section structure, method novelty, reference integrity  
**Evidence base:** `docs/rebuild/P0_P1_RESULTS_INDEX.md`; `artifacts/results/rebuild/{p0,p1,fixed_hp,calibration,intervals}/`; `artifacts/results/b6/`; `artifacts/results/review/`  
**Date:** 12 November 2026

---

## Verdict

**Major revision (statistical reporting).** The statistical machinery is largely correct. The metric equations are standard, the 1,465/8/35/1,492 confusion matrix recomputes to P 0.9946, R 0.9767, F1 0.9855, MCC 0.9715, Table 8 matches the WP2 calibration artifact, Table 13 reproduces the two-level bootstrap to four decimals, and the conformal 0.944/0.685/1.000/0.320 values are the disjoint sensitivity the text describes.

What fails is consistency. Table 10 reports the original B6 run (the checkpoint Table 6 calls tuned) while Tables 7, 8 and 11 report the fixed-hp rebuild, so the same corpus metric appears with two values (pooled AUROC 0.475/0.582 vs 0.494/0.590; QA AUROC 0.549 vs 0.558; QA flag 97.4% vs 96.8%). The operating-point paragraph contains two numbers that do not survive recomputation: at the P0 threshold every QA row is flagged (100%, not 97.4%), and the m3 maximum target-Platt score is 0.355 (not 0.313), which the paper's own card reports as 35.4%. The Cox slope interval is the envelope of three per-seed intervals, not a CI for the 0.18 mean; McNemar is run on rows although the paper's own resampling unit is the item pair; and the Wilson interval is defined and claimed used but never reported.

None of this overturns the central negative result. Several findings are wrong numbers rather than presentation, and the protocol mixing makes the shift narrative ambiguous. The paper needs one protocol per claim, recomputed operating points, and intervals that match their estimands.

I verified the PDF rendering of Tables 8, 9, 12 and 13 against the tex; the numbers match. No equation is algebraically wrong; the conformal quantile is under-specified (S9). The problems are in what the numbers are attached to.

## Reviewer's note

The paper is unusually well audited: the number-audit scripts cover most reported values, and the two-level bootstrap, the confusion matrix and the calibration table are reproducible from the artifacts. The failures above are localized reporting failures, not a broken experiment. If the three fixes are applied, the statistical claims will be internally consistent and traceable, and the central negative result (a domain-specific risk score, not a general-purpose detector) will stand on a clean quantitative record.
