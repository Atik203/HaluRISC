# Section 1 review: Abstract and Introduction

**Scope.** `Journal_Paper/halurisc.tex` lines 270-294, from `\begin{abstract}` through the end of `\section{Introduction}`.

**Evidence used.** Body Tables 6-11 and 15-17, Sections 5.2-5.8, `artifacts/results/rebuild/calibration/calibration_report.json` (`recall_at_5pct_fpr = 0.9949`, variant `m3`), the author names in `Journal_Paper/ref.bib`, and `Journal_Paper/out/halurisc.log` (28 pages; no undefined citations or references; only font-substitution warnings). The current abstract is 280 words.

## Verdict

**Minor revision.** No Critical issues: every headline number in the abstract traces to a body table or artifact. The blockers are two framing problems (the task-shift "collapse" and the judge comparison), one broken abstract sentence (the conformal result), and a cluster of undefined terms in the introduction (`m3`, "black-box model", "artifact-control", "group-aware"). All fixes are textual; no new experiments, numbers, or references are required.

## Strengths

- The section is unusually honest about negative results. The abstract ends on "EC-XGB is a domain-specific risk score, not a general-purpose detector," and it reports the length confound, MedHallu ECE 0.493, and conformal positive coverage 0.685 rather than burying them in Section 5.
- The four-paragraph introduction follows the G4 order: P1 current state, P2 literature plus per-paper gaps, P3 gap/objective/approach, P4 scope/contribution. Each paragraph has a discernible job, and the P2-to-P3 link (FaithBench's calibration gap to the four-issue list) works.
- The abstract follows the six-question order and stays inside the 250-300 word band. The headline numbers are the right ones: HaluEval saturation, the lexical/length ablation, MedHallu ranking versus calibration, the conformal shortfall, and the judge stress test. Q5 (why these datasets) is the thinnest answer, but the body's Section 4.2 covers it.
- The prose is specific and mostly student-simple. It names datasets and models, gives real numbers, and avoids generic "studies show" attribution. The cited studies get specific findings and specific lacks rather than one-line praise.
- The seven keywords cover topic (hallucination risk estimation), method (EC-XGB, conformal prediction, probability calibration, SHAP), problem (distribution shift), and domain (question answering). The title and abstract terminology is consistent.

## Text weight and readability

The abstract is 280 words in 13 sentences, a mean of 21.5 words per sentence, above the prompt's 14-17 median. The first half is well paced. The second half is a stack of result clauses: the training/evaluation sentence is 46 words with seven evaluation items, and the RAGTruth/conformal sentence is 34 words with six numbers. The proposed fixes add the missing context (0.944, EC-XGB's 0.310, the 158-row set) and split the long clauses, which brings the draft to about 298 words. That is inside the 300-word ceiling but with almost no margin. The 46-word evaluation list is the right place to recover words: 30 words can name the evaluation types, and Sections 4-5 carry the details.

The introduction paragraphs measure 157, 157, 227, and 169 raw tokens (including citations) and 8, 7, 10, and 8 sentences. P1 and P2 are readable but formulaic: P1 runs One/Another/A third, and P2 runs six finding-plus-lack sentences. The P1-to-P2 link is thematic rather than syntactic: P1 ends on the reviews' reliability call and P2 starts with six systems. The paragraph break carries it, but the meta-sentence is too weak to justify its length. P3 is the weight problem: 227 words in 10 sentences, five lists, and a cluster of undefined or inconsistent terms. Only the 7-word opening and the 9-word "Transfer limits" sentence are short; the rest are long, so the paragraph reads as one dense block. P4 is a second results section: it repeats the abstract's MedHallu and calibration numbers and adds two result pairs (RAGTruth adaptation, HaluEval recall) that the abstract does not need. Cutting the "Transfer limits" sentence, the seven-item evaluation list, the RAGTruth adaptation pair, and the recall clause would remove about 60 words without losing a headline claim, and would bring P3 and P4 close to the prompt's 2-7 sentence range. The best sentences in the section are the short, specific ones ("Judges carry high computational costs and encoder models are hard to interpret"; "The added evidence separates ranking from calibration"). Use that rhythm more often.

### Claims that pass

- **No general-detector claim.** The abstract ends "EC-XGB is a domain-specific risk score, not a general-purpose detector." The introduction's "separates ranking from calibration" supports the same scope. No sentence claims general hallucination detection.
