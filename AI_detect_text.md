## 1-Introduction

> Although the system is able to generate both ranked claims and their corresponding probabilities, there remains a disconnect between a ranking score that maintains relative order over time as well as maintaining confidence levels when the overall body of information has changed. This ranking score is then converted into a confidence level using Platt's calibration method. Additionally, Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) were used to provide supporting evidence or explanations for why the system chose a particular claim. Experiments also included evaluating the target application for calibration by checking how reliable the system's predictions were. Although the system was able to aggregate support based on individual explanations for each of the claims, the primary contributor to performance improvement on RAGTruth was due to the additional multi-source row data and the inclusion of a source indicator rather than the aggregation itself. In addition to creating transfer limits for RAGTruth and FaithBench, an additional new evaluation was added which includes MedHallu and a HaluBench subset with no overlap during training, the HaluEval task shift, conformal prediction, and judge stress tests. Each of these evaluations is reported using F1, ranking, calibration, and paired error metrics with the distinction made between ranking and calibration such that although the system transferred ranking performance to medical pairs, the system's associated probability did not transfer, and failed under the task shift.

## 3-2-Features

> Probabilities of NLI are derived from the cross encoder DeBERTa-v3 model which was trained on SNLI and MultiNLI. Semantic similarities are calculated by using embeddings from MiniLM to determine semantic similarities. Entities are identified using spaCy's named entity recognition (NER) tool. Since HaluEval labels answers that contain many words in addition to being longer as hallucinations more often than others, the length of an answer can be considered as a strong indicator of whether an answer has been labeled as a hallucination or not. Although lexical overlap is the most significant SHAP feature indicating copy and paste behavior, this does not indicate when someone copies the information into their response in a way that contradicts the original evidence while still paraphrasing. The NLI features are the only group that provides logic supporting the evidence rather than simply providing some form of overlap.

## 3-4-Calibration-and-Explainability

> Global Feature Importance is defined as the average absolute SHAP Value over a Stratified Test Sample. The Explanation Endpoint will output five features with the highest absolute SHAP Values for a specific prediction. While post-hoc attribution scores may be unstable even though there is a stable underlying model, this pilot evaluates them directly. A 20 case pilot assesses the impact of six operators: replacement of entities, numbers and dates, removal of support, addition of context and shuffling clauses. This pilot measures four metrics; the mean absolute score change, the fraction of large changes, the top-1 flip rate, and Spearman Rank Correlation Coefficient between the original and perturbed rankings.

## 4-2-Dataset

> The data was partitioned using a 70/15/15 ratio based on items, so both answers corresponding to an identical question will remain grouped together. MedHallu pqa_labeled provides medical QA pairs, along with human-annotated gold answers and paired LLM hallucinations. Both count measures (length) for m1 and subsequent versions are log-transformed (log1p), whereas in version m0 and the baseline version, raw counts are applied. Each claim is segmented into no more than six clause-level segments. Conjunctions are separated into separate clause levels. If the context exceeds four sentences, each claim will be mapped to its four most similar sentences according to token overlap and both sides of each pair will be truncated to 400 characters. An answer from a RAGTruth is classified as positive when it includes at least one annotated hallucination span. Worst-severity annotations are condensed using a worst-severity principle from FaithBench and questions that have a "Questionable" annotation are classified as positive. The label scheme used by HaluEval is unique. Both NLI and MiniLM inference were conducted on the reference GPU in fp16 format. Lexical and entity features were calculated on the CPU. Baseline versions that use feature-based logistic standardize their inputs on the training split. Sparse features are used by TF-IDF models and XGBoost does not scale. Values for the source indicator will be set to either 1 for FaithBench or MedHallu or 0 for HaluBench and the task-shift sets.
