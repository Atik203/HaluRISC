Explainable Hallucination Risk Prediction via Classical Machine Learning: A High-Efficiency, Single-Pass Architecture
Research Motivation and Problem Context
As Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly integrated into critical, high-stakes infrastructure spanning medical decision-support, legal precedent analysis, and autonomous enterprise systems, their inherent probabilistic nature introduces a severe deployment barrier: the generation of hallucinations1. Hallucinations manifest as fluent, syntactically coherent text that is factually inaccurate, logically inconsistent, or entirely ungrounded in provided reference material4. Because modern generative architectures are optimized for linguistic plausibility and sequence continuation rather than strict factual fidelity, they frequently produce confident fabrications that can mislead end-users, corrupt downstream automated pipelines, and introduce profound operational liabilities2.
The current industry standard for mitigating this risk relies almost exclusively on computationally expensive verification frameworks, predominantly "LLM-as-a-judge" mechanisms or multi-sampling consistency checks7. In the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm, a secondary, often larger model is tasked with evaluating the primary model's output for semantic entailment and factual correctness10. While this approach can process complex contextual nuances, it essentially doubles the required API calls, drastically inflating operational costs and introducing severe latency bottlenecks12. Similarly, sampling-based techniques, such as SelfCheckGPT or semantic entropy probes, rely on generating multiple diverse responses to a single prompt to measure output variance7. These multi-generation frameworks impose a five-to-tenfold increase in inference latency, rendering them completely unviable for real-time applications such as voice-activated agents or high-throughput enterprise routing, where zero-latency cost is a strict engineering mandate13.
The fundamental problem dictates a critical inquiry: how can factual hallucinations in LLM outputs be detected in real-time utilizing only a single text pass, while maintaining a high classification accuracy characterized by an -score exceeding 0.75? Furthermore, because trust is the ultimate currency of AI adoption, this high-speed detection must not operate as an opaque system. The detection architecture must provide transparent, interpretable, and human-readable feature attributions that explicitly justify the calculated hallucination risk to the end-user17.
Literature Review and Novelty Contribution
The landscape of hallucination detection has evolved rapidly, currently bifurcating into neural-heavy verification techniques and emerging classical machine learning approaches. A comprehensive review of the 2025–2026 literature demonstrates a pronounced paradigm shift toward utilizing classical machine learning classifiers operating on surface-level linguistic features, bypassing the need for deep neural processing during the detection phase18.
Recent studies utilizing the Hallucination Evaluation for Large Language Models (HaluEval) benchmark have established that multi-signal approaches—combining lexical overlap, entity coverage, and semantic similarity—enable classical classifiers to achieve highly competitive discrimination metrics20. Foundational work in this domain demonstrates that a linear Support Vector Machine (SVM) utilizing 30,000 unigram TF-IDF features augmented with structural overlap metrics can achieve an Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUROC) of 0.835 and an -score of 0.751 on the HaluEval dataset18. More advanced implementations utilizing tree-based ensembles have pushed these boundaries further. Research applying Random Forest classifiers to semantic context uptake features has reported AUROCs of 0.8669, achieving this with a 1000x faster inference speed (5 milliseconds versus 5 seconds) compared to traditional retrieval-based neural models14. Concurrently, gradient boosting frameworks, specifically XGBoost, have been successfully deployed to integrate multi-granular uncertainty features across token-level and sequence-level dimensions, reaching AUROCs of 89.27% while reducing latency by a factor of 8.2x compared to semantic entropy methods15.
Despite these advances in speed and accuracy, current lightweight solutions overwhelmingly lack real-time user explainability. While classical models generate rapid scalar probabilities, they do not intrinsically explain which linguistic artifacts triggered the hallucination flag. In parallel, Explainable AI (XAI) research emphasizes that post-hoc interpretability is mandatory for establishing user trust4. Techniques such as SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) have been utilized in medical and financial ML domains to attribute predictions to specific input features, offering both numerical and visual interpretability23. However, applying SHAP to LLM outputs has historically required access to internal token probabilities or attention matrices, rendering them useless for proprietary, black-box LLMs where internal parameters are hidden behind commercial APIs4.
This research bridges the critical gap between high-accuracy classification and real-time interpretability. The novelty of the proposed framework lies in coupling a highly optimized XGBoost/CatBoost classifier—reliant entirely on surface-level, black-box feature extraction—with an interactive SHAP visualization engine. This delivers an end-to-end system that not only flags hallucinations in milliseconds but explicitly visualizes the structural and semantic reasoning behind the risk score, thereby transforming opaque detection into actionable, transparent intelligence.
Foundational Design Decisions and Assumptions
To engineer a system capable of bypassing expensive neural evaluators, the architecture is grounded in specific theoretical assumptions and strict operational design parameters.
The primary theoretical foundation is the Black-Box Hypothesis. This hypothesis posits that hallucinations inevitably leave statistical, syntactic, and linguistic "fingerprints" within the generated surface text27. While internal state probing—analyzing hidden state trajectories across transformer layers—can accurately detect divergence between parametric knowledge and external context, it requires full access to model weights30. The black-box hypothesis assumes that these internal conflicts manifest externally through observable phenomena such as unnatural token length distributions, abrupt semantic shifts, lack of explicit citations, and anomalous entity density18. Consequently, the system assumes that these external fingerprints can be isolated and quantified without requiring the target LLM’s internal token probabilities, enabling universal application across both open-source and proprietary commercial models32.
The second foundational assumption relates to compute efficiency. The architecture operates on the premise that supervised, tree-based machine learning ensembles will achieve classification performance comparable to large neural evaluators while slashing computational overhead (measured in FLOPs) and inference latency by at least an order of magnitude7. Production-grade environments highlight that classical classifiers can execute hallucination detection at a cost of $0.23 per 1,000 tasks, compared to $7.50 when utilizing GPT-4 as an evaluator, while maintaining sub-20 millisecond p95 latency34.
Operationally, the system is strictly constrained to single-pass extraction. Multi-sampling techniques are explicitly excluded from the architecture. While generating five independent responses to evaluate consistency is highly effective for identifying generative variance, it inherently defeats the primary objective of real-time, low-cost detection7. The pipeline is restricted to processing the single initial prompt, the provided context, and the single generated answer. Furthermore, the core processing pipeline is designed to execute entirely within local environments, utilizing optimized hardware-agnostic frameworks (such as a WSL-based Miniconda configuration). This ensures total isolated dependency management, prevents the introduction of external API latency during the evaluation phase, and guarantees strict reproducibility for peer review and academic benchmarking.
System Pipeline Architecture
The hallucination detection framework operates sequentially via a highly decoupled, asynchronous microservice architecture. This design allows the computationally lightweight extraction and classification layers to process data rapidly before passing the resulting vectors to the explainability and presentation layers.
The processing sequence initiates at the Input Layer, where an end-user or downstream application submits a standardized JSON payload containing the user query, any relevant grounding context, and the candidate LLM answer via a React/TypeScript frontend. This payload is transmitted to the Feature Extraction Layer, constructed upon a high-throughput Python FastAPI backend. Here, the raw text is subjected to intense natural language processing utilizing optimized, C-backed libraries. The text is parsed, tokenized, and analyzed to extract structural, semantic, and linguistic features, dynamically constructing a high-dimensional numerical feature vector representative of the text's latent hallucination risk.
Following extraction, the vector enters the Classification Layer. An optimized XGBoost or CatBoost gradient boosting model ingests the numerical array. By evaluating the complex, non-linear interactions between the linguistic and structural features, the ensemble model outputs a raw margin score, which is subsequently calibrated into a definitive probability score representing the likelihood of hallucination.
Simultaneously, the feature vector and the classification model are passed to the Explainability Layer. Utilizing the shap library's TreeExplainer algorithm, the system computes the Shapley values for the specific inference25. This process calculates the exact marginal contribution of every individual feature to the final prediction, isolating the specific variables—such as an uncharacteristically high noun-to-verb ratio or a stark mismatch in named entities—that influenced the classifier.
In the final Presentation Layer, the backend serializes both the calibrated probability score and the array of SHAP attributions into a response payload. The React frontend consumes this data, dynamically rendering a visual "Trust Gauge" scaled from 0% to 100%. Directly below the gauge, the application generates a sorted, color-coded waterfall or bar chart of the SHAP values. This visualization provides immediate, granular insight, explicitly demonstrating to the user exactly why the LLM response was deemed trustworthy or flagged as a potential fabrication.
Detailed Feature Extraction Strategy
The efficacy of classical machine learning in natural language tasks is entirely dependent on the descriptive density of the feature vector. Because the system bypasses deep neural processing, it must extract highly discriminative surface heuristics that act as proxies for internal model uncertainty and factual divergence27. The extraction layer targets four primary dimensions of linguistic and statistical behavior.
The first dimension focuses on Structural and Syntactic Markers. Hallucinated generations frequently exhibit structural anomalies when compared to factually grounded text26. The pipeline extracts foundational metrics including total sentence count, absolute token length, average word length, and punctuation density. Furthermore, the system executes rapid Part-of-Speech (POS) tagging to generate syntactic distribution ratios. Research indicates that models generating fabrications often alter their grammatical structure, relying heavily on syntactic padding, manifesting as anomalous noun-to-verb ratios or an over-reliance on specific modifiers to bridge factual gaps26. Identifying overly complex or unnaturally uniform sentence structures provides a strong baseline indicator of generative instability.
The second dimension evaluates Information Density and Entity Overlap. Intrinsic hallucinations occur when the generated output contradicts or fabricates information not present in the source material2. The most explicit surface manifestation of this failure is a mismatch in specific entities. The system utilizes Named Entity Recognition (NER) to isolate and count organizations, geographical locations, persons, and temporal dates within both the grounded context and the LLM response20. The feature array calculates a strict entity overlap ratio. If the LLM introduces a high density of novel entities that lack antecedents in the prompt or context, it strongly signals that the model is relying on unverified parametric memory rather than grounded facts, elevating the hallucination risk19.
The third dimension quantifies Linguistic Uncertainty and Hedging. While some LLMs exhibit "confident fabrication" where they hallucinate with absolute linguistic certainty, uncertainty frequently leaks into the surface text through subtle epistemic hedging28. The extraction pipeline runs a frequency analysis against a curated lexicon of uncertainty markers (e.g., "might," "possibly," "it is believed," "could suggest"). These frequencies are normalized against the total response length to prevent length-bias. A high concentration of hedging terminology, particularly when correlated with low entity overlap, acts as a powerful discriminator for text generated under high internal uncertainty36.
The final dimension addresses Semantic Consistency and Contextual Drift. To capture broad semantic alignment beyond exact keyword matching, the system computes the cosine similarity between the dense mathematical embeddings of the prompt/context and the LLM response20. By utilizing lightweight, highly optimized sentence-transformers (such as all-MiniLM-L6-v2), the system maps the texts into a shared vector space, quantifying semantic drift without violating strict latency constraints. Features such as unigram TF-IDF overlap ratios and explicit context-adherence metrics provide a quantifiable measurement of how tightly the generated answer clings to the provided evidence, exposing outputs that drift into irrelevant or fabricated topical domains14.

Technology Stack and Implementation Tooling
The successful execution of a sub-50ms, single-pass classifier requires a highly optimized, interoperable technology stack designed to eliminate execution bottlenecks at every layer.
The backend core is executed in Python 3.10+, leveraging the scikit-learn ecosystem for data standardization and calibration mapping. The primary classification engines rely on xgboost and catboost. These libraries bypass native Python overhead by executing optimized C/C++ compiled algorithms, making them ideal for ultra-low latency inference environments where rapid gradient boosting evaluation is mandatory24.
For Natural Language Processing, the system strictly utilizes spaCy for all structural extraction, Part-of-Speech tagging, and Named Entity Recognition. Because spaCy is a Cython-backed library, it operates at speeds significantly higher than deep-learning native libraries (such as HuggingFace Transformers), allowing document parsing to occur in single-digit milliseconds42. Lexical frequency analysis, particularly against the epistemic hedging dictionaries, is handled via nltk.
The explainability layer is powered by the shap library, specifically utilizing the TreeExplainer module. This algorithm is mathematically tailored for tree ensembles, allowing it to compute the exact Shapley values in polynomial time rather than the exponential complexity required for exact model-agnostic Shapley computation, preserving the real-time nature of the detection pipeline25.
The API gateway is constructed using FastAPI combined with the uvicorn ASGI server. This configuration ensures completely asynchronous request handling, preventing concurrent feature extraction requests from blocking the main execution thread, thereby enabling high-throughput enterprise scalability. Finally, the presentation layer is built utilizing React and TypeScript. Data visualization is managed by the Recharts library, which seamlessly parses the JSON arrays containing the SHAP values into dynamic, interactive waterfall charts, bridging the gap between raw algorithmic output and user comprehension.
Dataset Engineering and Prior Distributions
The training of a robust classical classifier is inherently bounded by the quality, scale, and distribution of its training data. The primary environment for model training and validation is the Hallucination Evaluation for Large Language Models (HaluEval) benchmark21. HaluEval is a massive, high-quality corpus containing 64,507 labeled examples specifically curated to evaluate hallucination recognition across highly diverse generative constraints18.
The dataset engineering plan leverages the unique structural advantages of the HaluEval splits. The corpus is divided into task-specific generative domains and generalized queries, each exhibiting vastly different prior probability distributions.

The task-specific splits (Question Answering, Dialogue, and Summarization) are perfectly balanced, containing precisely 50% hallucinated and 50% factual responses. These subsets are constructed by generating paired outputs for the same source context, providing an ideal, unbiased environment for the XGBoost model to learn the differential features of intrinsic and extrinsic hallucinations without the interference of prior probability skew18.
Conversely, the General User Queries subset consists of human-annotated samples reflecting a much more realistic, imbalanced distribution, presenting a hallucination rate of approximately 18.08%19. This highly imbalanced split is absolutely critical for the final calibration phase. Global models trained exclusively on artificially balanced data inevitably become systematically overconfident and produce severe false-positive rates when deployed into real-world, imbalanced environments18. The General Queries subset is utilized strictly during the Platt Scaling phase to correct the probability margins and ensure the ECE remains minimal across realistic operational deployment.
Evaluation Strategy and Robustness Metrics
Because the architecture must ultimately perform reliably in imbalanced environments, relying solely on raw classification accuracy is a fundamentally invalid evaluation approach. The classifier's efficacy must be proven through a comprehensive matrix of discrimination, robustness, and computational efficiency metrics.
The primary threshold-dependent metrics will rely on Precision, Recall, and the -score. In the context of automated hallucination detection, Precision is heavily weighted to minimize false positives; an over-sensitive detector that repeatedly flags factually correct answers as hallucinations rapidly degrades user trust and creates severe alert fatigue. The -score provides the necessary harmonic mean to ensure the model balances detection capability with rigorous precision.
To evaluate the model's fundamental discriminative capability independent of chosen probability thresholds, the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (AUROC) will serve as the primary robustness metric18. A target AUROC exceeding 0.83 indicates a highly robust capacity to separate factual and hallucinated distributions. Furthermore, to explicitly account for the true and false positive/negative ratios within the highly imbalanced (18%) general user query set, the Matthews Correlation Coefficient () will be calculated. Unlike the -score,  considers all four quadrants of the confusion matrix, providing a more reliable statistical rate for imbalanced datasets.
Finally, the architecture must prove its foundational hypothesis regarding compute efficiency. End-to-end inference latency, measured in milliseconds from API payload receipt to SHAP JSON response, will be strictly benchmarked. These latency metrics, alongside overall computational overhead (FLOPs), will be juxtaposed against heavy LLM-as-a-judge baselines to empirically validate the operational superiority of the single-pass classical methodology7.
Edge Cases and Failure Mitigation
The intentional reliance on surface-level textual heuristics rather than internal neural probing introduces specific architectural vulnerabilities. The system must account for edge cases where statistical linguistic markers break down or present deceptive signals.
The first critical edge case involves ultra-short responses. If an LLM output consists of minimal tokens—for example, responding to a complex query with a simple "Yes," "No," or a single integer—the text processing layer will fail to generate sufficient data. At this scale, linguistic hedging, syntactic density, and part-of-speech ratios become mathematically undefined or present as extreme, sparse outliers38. To mitigate this, the FastAPI layer implements a strict minimum token threshold. Responses falling below this limit trigger an immediate processing bypass, returning a "Not Enough Data" (N/A) state to the React frontend. This hard logic gate prevents the XGBoost model from attempting to extrapolate patterns from statistical noise and assigning high-confidence hallucination scores to benign, concise answers.
The second, highly deceptive edge case is the phenomenon of "confident fabrication." Contemporary research on internal LLM dynamics reveals that models can suffer from "overthinking" or "confounder propagation" in early decoding layers, leading them to generate profound falsehoods with absolute linguistic certainty28. In these instances, the output text is completely devoid of epistemic hedging or structural uncertainty, which would normally trigger the linguistic classifiers37. To combat confident fabrications, the architecture relies heavily on the multidimensional nature of its feature vector. Even if the linguistic uncertainty features score a zero, the model relies on the counterbalancing weight of Information Density and Semantic Consistency. An LLM confidently fabricating details will, by definition, introduce novel Named Entities resulting in a severe NER mismatch, and the resulting text will deviate semantically from the grounded context. This triggers the XGBoost model to flag the response based on severe contextual drift and entity fabrication, effectively bypassing the deceptive lack of syntactic hedging19.

Implementation Roadmap and Execution Order
To guarantee seamless integration across the machine learning, backend, and frontend domains without encountering architectural blockers, development must adhere to a strict sequential implementation order, optimized for a comprehensive research timeline.
During the initial phase, efforts are completely isolated to Data Ingestion and Model Engineering. Development begins with the Data Ingestion Hub, implementing robust JSONL parsers to load, clean, and standardize the HaluEval splits into memory-efficient dataframes. Following ingestion, the Feature Factory is constructed; the extract_features(text) function is written and rigorously unit-tested to ensure it reliably transforms string inputs into structured numerical arrays, flawlessly handling complex NLP tasks like POS tagging and NER extraction. Once the feature pipeline is stable, Model Training commences. Baseline models (Logistic Regression) are fit to establish a performance floor, followed by the rigorous hyperparameter tuning of the XGBoost and CatBoost models. The models are evaluated against the held-out test splits, generating the baseline cross-validation metrics, and the optimal weights are serialized to disk14.
The second phase bridges the machine learning core to the interactive user experience. Development pivots to the Explainability Engine, implementing the SHAP TreeExplainer over the serialized model to ensure that marginal feature contributions compute correctly and sum exactly to the raw output margin. Subsequently, the API Gateway is erected; the extraction, classification, and explanation functions are wrapped within asynchronous FastAPI endpoints, establishing POST routes that accept raw text and return structured JSON predictions. Finally, the Dashboard is developed. The React/TypeScript frontend connects to the local API, implementing the data visualization libraries necessary to parse the SHAP arrays and render the interactive Trust Gauge, finalizing the end-to-end artifact.
Post-Course Publication and Benchmarking Strategy
To elevate this research from a theoretical prototype to a robust, peer-reviewed contribution to the computational linguistics community, a rigorous publication strategy is required. Following initial validation, the methodology is designed to scale directly into a manuscript suitable for high-impact informatics journals, explicitly targeting publications such as IEEE Access or Elsevier's Expert Systems with Applications43.
The core of the publication strategy involves executing exhaustive feature ablation studies. By systematically removing specific extraction dimensions (e.g., completely removing POS tags, or disabling NER overlap counts) and measuring the subsequent degradation in AUROC and -scores, the research will empirically prove the individual discriminative worth of each linguistic heuristic16. Furthermore, to demonstrate that the model is not merely overfitted to the syntactic quirks of HaluEval, the classifier will be benchmarked against out-of-distribution datasets, including medical and legal subsets from TruthfulQA and RAGTruth, proving cross-domain generalization7. To ensure compliance with open-science mandates, the complete frontend, API, and model weights will be containerized via Docker and published as a public GitHub repository, providing the global academic community with a fully reproducible, zero-shot hallucination detection framework.
Expected Research Outcomes and Final Synthesis
This research blueprint provides a comprehensive trajectory for developing a high-efficiency, single-pass hallucination classifier capable of directly challenging the dominance of complex neural evaluators. Based on the established performance of classical machine learning applied to the HaluEval corpus, the engineered system is fully expected to achieve an AUROC exceeding 0.83 and an -score greater than 0.7518.
Crucially, the architecture will execute these evaluations at a mere fraction of the computational and financial costs associated with LLM-as-a-judge frameworks. By bypassing secondary generation loops and external API calls, the system is projected to achieve sub-50ms inference times, satisfying the stringent latency constraints required for real-time, high-stakes deployments7. The ultimate, tangible output of this research will be a fully functional, locally hosted web application. By successfully synthesizing the brute-force efficiency of XGBoost with the transparent, mathematical rigor of SHAP feature attribution, this architecture visually and functionally bridges the critical gap between complex machine learning interpretability and the paramount necessity for end-user trust in generative AI systems.
